Comprehensive Business Description

Run My AI Business, operated by Jacqui Abrams and supported by the Run My AI Biz newsletter on Substack, is an AI-focused educational publishing and independent reseller business. It helps aspiring entrepreneurs, solopreneurs, digital product creators, and small business owners explore how artificial intelligence can support the creation, launch, and ongoing management of online businesses. Its content emphasizes practical applications, approachable explanations, and conversational workflows that allow users to direct AI in everyday language without needing to learn coding.

The business addresses a common challenge for entrepreneurs: bringing together the many pieces needed to turn an idea into a functioning business. Its educational focus spans business planning, AI-assisted product development, digital product creation, sales copy, sales pages, lead magnets, sales funnels, email follow-up, scheduling, and recurring operational tasks. Rather than presenting these as disconnected activities, Run My AI Business explores how specialized AI assistants can work together around the owner’s business goals.

Educational Content and Newsletter

Run My AI Biz is the business’s weekly Substack newsletter. It provides sourced AI tool comparisons, pricing breakdowns, explanations of AI’s development, step-by-step example businesses, and quick-start ideas. Its purpose is to help readers understand what different tools do, how they fit into an online business, and how to move from researching possibilities to taking practical action. The supplied business description lists a free starting option and a paid subscription of $9 per month for access to full weekly issues.

Topics include AI for entrepreneurs, online business automation, digital products, lead generation, sales funnels, and ongoing business management. Educational examples explore how an idea can become an offer, how marketing materials can support that offer, and how workflows can be adapted to different audiences and selling platforms.

The newsletter complements, but remains separate from, the external mastermind Jacqui promotes. It provides ongoing educational content, while the mastermind offers demonstrations, business examples, and resources for developing specialized AI assistants.

AI Business Mastermind and 38-Bot Resource Library

As an independent reseller, Jacqui promotes a separate AI business mastermind featuring three entrepreneurs who share examples of their business workflows and AI assistants. An accompanying resource, The 38 Bot Empire, documents 38 bot examples covering business planning, research, content production, marketing, sales, website development, and operations. These examples illustrate how specialized assistants can support different functions within one business or provide starting points for exploring several business models.

A central feature is access to customizable master prompts—the written instructions that define an assistant’s role, responsibilities, workflow, and approval requirements. Participants can adapt these instructions to their own niche, audience, products, brand voice, and objectives rather than developing every workflow from scratch. Many prompts appear directly in the guide; others are referenced in separate “Private Stash” materials or supporting files. The collection is therefore a library of adaptable bot blueprints and resources, rather than 38 identical, independently functioning software products.

Business Applications and Use Cases

The resource library includes examples across several business functions

  • Business planning and coordination: chief-of-staff assistants, business idea evaluation, opportunity research, project tracking, and coordination between specialized bots.
  • Newsletter publishing and growth: topic research, issue drafting, local and paid newsletters, subscriber acquisition strategies, and local sponsorship prospecting.
  • Digital products and education: course planning, presentation development, lesson scripts, video creation, video editing, and product launch preparation.
  • Affiliate marketing and sales funnels: offer research, lead magnets, opt-in pages, bridge pages, email sequences, advertising drafts, and campaign planning.
  • Website and software development: local business websites, professional directories, hosting workflows, custom software projects, and search engine optimization.
  • Social media and communities: Facebook content, X posts and threads, newsletter repurposing, and content development for paid communities.
  • Marketing strategy and conversion: competitor research, customer research, offer positioning, landing-page analysis, and webinar development.
  • Business administration: billing monitoring, overdue-payment reminders, first-line support workflows, and server-health reporting.

Owners can select individual assistants for specific needs or combine several into a coordinated process. For example, an opportunity-research bot could identify a topic, a course-building bot could develop educational materials, a video bot could support production, and a launch bot could help prepare the sales funnel. Other combinations could support a local newsletter, website service, professional directory, or affiliate marketing project. These are possible applications—not a claim that every bot represents a separate profitable business.

Customization, Coordination, and Human Oversight

The approach emphasizes adapting workflows to the user’s circumstances. Participants supply their business context, review drafts, request changes, and refine instructions over time. A chief-of-staff-style assistant can act as the main point of contact, helping organize projects and route tasks to other specialized bots.

Human-directed automation is a recurring principle. The guide instructs users to retain safeguards requiring approval before bots send messages, publish content, spend money, or sign up for services. Individual workflows also include review checkpoints, secure account connections, source verification, and restrictions on inventing statistics, proof, or income claims. Successful execution depends on the underlying AI platform, integrations, permissions, and supporting services—not on a prompt alone.

Although the collection supports many possible projects, its recommended starting approach is focused: identify relevant workflows, build one assistant, use it, and then expand. This allows owners to develop manageable systems before attempting to coordinate multiple projects or businesses.

Audience and Business Model

Run My AI Business serves first-time entrepreneurs, professionals developing side businesses, digital product creators, and existing business owners seeking to reduce repetitive work. Its messaging particularly addresses people who feel overwhelmed by technical instructions, disconnected software, or the number of tasks involved in launching and maintaining an online business.

The business combines subscription-based educational publishing with independent resale of the external mastermind. Jacqui’s role is to introduce and promote the program, explain potential applications, and share her perspective as a user and reseller. She earns commissions through program referrals but is not the creator of the promoted AI technology or one of the mastermind’s presenters.

Positioning and Value Proposition

Run My AI Business brings together accessible AI education and an introduction to customizable business-automation resources. Its central value proposition is helping people move beyond isolated chatbot questions toward repeatable, task-specific workflows that support business creation and ongoing operations.

The newsletter provides continuing education and practical ideas, while the mastermind’s bot resources offer examples and adaptable instructions. Together, these can help owners explore a venture, strengthen an existing operation, or coordinate several related projects while retaining control over decisions, spending, and publication.

The business presents these applications as educational opportunities rather than guaranteed outcomes. The bot guide itself distinguishes some demonstrated workflows from experimental or not-yet-used examples, so the collection should not be portrayed as 38 proven income-producing businesses.
